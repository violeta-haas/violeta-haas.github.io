  • Ask questions early rather than sitting with confusion

Discussing sensitive material

  • Use careful, scholarly language when discussing hate speech and targets of violence
  • If something lands badly, say so. We can pause and restart

Note

Content warning: This module examines the causes, consequences and responses to bias-motivated behavior. Materials may include descriptions of hate crimes, hate speech, and other disturbing or offensive content (including racist, antisemitic, Islamophobic, anti-LGBTQ+, and similarly hateful language or imagery).

Assessment

Three components

Component Weight Core idea
Participation 30% Weekly Comments + attendance + seminar engagement
Position paper 10% Express and defend an opinion based on the readings
Research proposal 60% Propose an empirical study

Participation (30%)

  1. Weekly comments (150–250 words), due Sunday 11:59 pm
    • Focus on required readings
    • Critique, question, or reflect. Do not summarise.
    • No comment in Week 1, Reading Week, or your position-paper week
    • Two additional jokers
    • Week 11: Guest lecturer paper comments cannot be skipped
  2. Attendance: regular & prepared
  3. In-seminar contributions

Position paper (10%)

  • 800–1,200 words on one week’s readings
  • Defend a position: (dis)agree with key arguments, usefulness for current politics, or take sides when papers conflict
  • Due Sunday 11:59 pm before that week’s Monday seminar
  • Replaces the weekly comment that week

Research proposal (60%)

A proposal for an empirical study (2,500–3,000 words):

  1. Clear research question
  2. Critical literature synthesis
  3. Theoretical argument
  4. Empirical approach (cases, methods, data)

Sign up

Book a 15-minute office hour (in person or Zoom) in the two weeks after Reading Week to discuss your topic.

You do not need to carry out the full study. Deadline: Fri 11 Dec, 11:59 pm.

Research proposal (60%)

A typical table of contents for the research paper might look like this:

  1. Introduction
  2. Theoretical motivation
  3. Design
  4. Data description
  5. Results
  6. Interpretation and conclusions

 

Written work

  • Stay within word limits (references / captions don’t count)
  • PDF, double-spaced, 12pt
  • Consistent citation style + bibliography
  • Research proposal: short end note on gender share of cited authors (e.g. for this syllabus: Cited authors: 37% women among all authors; 44% women among first authors. About 60% of readings include at least one woman. Reflection only, not graded.)

Late work and extensions

Late Submission of Assessments (Extensions) Policy

  • Late work without approved extension: 5 points per day (including weekends)
  • More than five days late without approval: may not be marked
  • Once work has been marked and returned to others: late submissions get 0
  • Request extensions for extenuating circumstances before the deadline

LENS (flexible deadlines)

If your LENS includes flexible assessment deadlines, you get a default one-week extension on eligible written work where practical.

  • Position paper and research proposal: applies automatically
  • Weekly comments: not practical (read before Monday). You get an extra joker instead. Contact me if you need another arrangement.

Useful Resources: Trinity Disability Service

Academic integrity and plagiarism

  • All submitted work must be your own, plagiarism of any kind will be sanctioned:
    • unacknowledged quotation or paraphrase
    • self-plagiarism (e.g. reusing work from another module)
    • ghostwriting
    • presenting AI text as your own unassisted work

Academic dishonesty can mean no credit for an assignment, a failing grade for the module, or further College sanctions

How to read for this seminar

When preparing a paper, try to identify:

  1. Research question
  2. What is explained (Y)
  3. Main explanatory factor (X)
  4. Central concepts
  5. Methods / research design
  1. Main findings
  2. Real-world implications
  3. Contribution to the literature
